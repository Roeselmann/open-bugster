# Tickets

A ticket is the one unit of work in Open-Bugster, from a single bug to a whole campaign: what tells them apart is the ticket's type, its subtickets, and its schedule. On a board it is a card: a title, a description, an internal comment, priority, due date, build number, any number of links to things elsewhere, a to-do list, attachments, one category, any number of labels, an assignee, a comment thread, and a history. Imported TestFlight feedback and Jira issues become tickets too, with the original submission or issue attached. The build number appears in the editor, only on boards with TestFlight connected. The card's footer keeps it short: a line with the first link and the comment count, when there are any, and below it the due date on the left, the assignee and the ticket number on the right; who filed it and when stay in the editor. A card whose due date comes near gets an orange border, then a red one, which stays while it is overdue — unless the ticket sits in a lane marked Done; how many days ahead each colour starts is set per board under **Board settings → Board** (defaults: orange 7 days before, red 1 day before). As soon as any of your boards has a ticket with a due date, or a live ticket with the end of its live window, an alarm clock appears in the header: grey with the number of dates while none is lit, red with the number of orange and red ones once any is. It opens every one of them, grouped by workspace and board, due dates as dots and live windows as rings, linking to each board and opening the ticket itself. Tickets are moved by dragging, archived instead of deleted, and numbered instance-wide.

**Schedule.** How a ticket sits in time is one of four, picked in the **Schedule** block of the editor:

- **None**: a plain ticket, no dates at all.
- **Milestone**: one day, an event such as a meeting or a go-live. It has a single *Date*, no start and no live window, shows a diamond on the card and on the timeline.
- **Lifecycle**: work from a start date to a due date, then a live window. A due date alone is a plain deadline; start and due on the same day make a one-day bar.
- **Recurring**: the same card comes round again and again, like a series in a calendar: a weekly review, a monthly report. See *Recurring tickets* below.

The icon in a card's date says which it is: an alarm clock for a deadline, a diamond for a milestone, the repeat arrows for a recurring ticket, an antenna for a ticket with a live window.

Switching clears what the new schedule has no room for, in one save: *None* every date and what the ticket waits on, *Milestone* the start and the live window, keeping the due date (or the start, if that was all there was) as its day. A milestone picked before it has a day is saved once the date is entered. The schedule is stored with the ticket; a ticket that gets a date without one, through the API or an import, becomes a lifecycle.

**Recurring tickets.** A recurring ticket repeats *every N* days, weeks, months or years, counted from *Starts on*, the day of the first round, which can be today: weekly rounds keep its weekday, monthly ones its day of the month (the last day in a shorter month), so a quarter is every 3 months. *Ends on*, beside it, stops the series; empty, it runs on. The line under the rule names the round to do next, or the one still open; it is the ticket's due date, with the usual warnings, and the clock moves it on by itself. A rule that puts the rounds on other days starts at its first round from today.

A round closes by itself on the day the next one is due. It counts as **done** when the card sits in a done or live lane or every to-do is ticked, and as **missed** otherwise. A missed round shows a red *Missed* mark on the card, a warning in the editor, an entry in the history, and goes out as the webhook `ticket.round_missed`. Then the next round starts: with *Fresh to-dos each round* (on by default) the plain to-dos are unticked and the decisions opened again (subtickets are tickets of their own and stay as they are), and the card goes back to the lane set under *Each new round goes to*. That way a round finished early can sit in the done lane until the next one, when it comes back on its own. **Done, next round** and **Skip, next round** close the round at once and start the next one after today; skipping is for a round that does not take place, a holiday say, and leaves no warning. A line under the buttons says what both will do: untick the to-dos, which lane the card goes to, which round comes next. **Undo** in the message that follows, or **Reopen** beside the last round in the list, opens that round again as it stood: its due date, the ticked to-dos and decisions, the lane and place of the card. That works for the last round only, a missed one too, and until the next round is due; a round closed before this existed gets back its date alone. The editor lists the last rounds, green for done, red for missed, grey for skipped, each with the to-dos it left open on hover. If the server was down over several rounds, only the round that is on now starts; the ones in between are not made up. On a timeline, past rounds are small diamonds in their colour and the coming ones hollow.

**Live window.** Apart from the days it is worked on, a ticket can have the days it is out in the world: *Live from* and *Live until*, in a lifecycle; **Clear** beside *Live from* removes both. For a campaign, the due date is its go-live and the live window its run; for an asset, the live window can end when its approval expires. It only lights up while the ticket sits in a live lane (see [boards.md](boards.md)), where the card shows "Live until 31 Jul", then "Ends in 3 days", then "Ended 2 days ago" in red. The end is never before the start.

**Waits on.** In the Schedule block a milestone or a lifecycle can name the tickets it waits on: finish-to-start, it starts once they have ended. **Add** searches the open tickets of the workspace, leaving out anything that already waits on this one, since that would close a loop; the × lets one go. Each row shows where that ticket stands and its due date, with **Late** in red when it is past due and not done, and **Ends later** in orange when it ends after this ticket starts. Below the list, **Waited on by** names the tickets waiting on this one, as far as you can see them. A finished ticket, in a done or live lane, holds nothing up any more. Switching the schedule to *None* lets go of what it waits on along with its dates.

**Shifting dates.** **Shift dates…** at the bottom of the Schedule block moves the ticket's start and due date by whole days, earlier with a minus, and every ticket waiting on it, directly or through others, by the same days. **Preview** lists what would move first; finished work and tickets on boards you may not edit stay where they are and stop the push there. Live windows never move: a run that follows the go-live is adjusted by hand.

**Where used.** A subticket's editor header has a second line: one pill per ticket that holds it, with the upward arrow, its title and its number; a click opens that ticket. Archived parents are struck through, parents on boards you cannot open are left out, and a ticket that is no subticket has no second line. It is the way up the hierarchy, as the subtickets in the to-dos are the way down.

**Start date and timeline.** Beside the due date a ticket can have a start date; together they span it across days. The start is never after the due date: moving one past the other in the editor drags the other along. With a start date the card shows the span ("Nov 9 – Dec 11") until the due date's countdown matters more. A ticket with subtickets shows a **Timeline** section below its to-dos: the range it covers, an **Open timeline** button and, when something is off, a small warnings link that folds the list open and shut. The button opens the chart over the whole window: the ticket and every subticket down through theirs as bars across their days, grouped and collapsible, with a line for today. A subticket on a board you cannot open is listed but not opened up. An arrow runs from the end of each ticket to the start of every ticket waiting on it, dashed orange where the waiting one starts too early. The live window runs as a thin pale band behind the work, red once it has ended while the ticket is still live. The section in the editor and the top of the chart warn about open tickets past their due date, live tickets past their live window, dependencies out of order or waiting on something late, and about anything that starts before or ends after the window of the ticket the timeline belongs to; that ticket's own dates are the commitment and are never widened to fit.

## Using it

### Creating and editing

New tickets are created with **Add ticket** at the bottom of a lane, or with the **+** in a lane's header to put one at the top; the editor lets you change the lane and the position before saving. A new ticket starts as the workspace's type "Ticket" (untyped where the workspace has none); the API and MCP leave a ticket untyped unless they name a type. Either way they land in that lane. An open ticket can be sent to the top or bottom of its lane with the two arrow buttons beside the **Lane** select. Imported tickets additionally show their origin: the TestFlight feedback with tester, device, system, locale, and build, or the Jira issue with key, project, type, status, priority, reporter, and assignee as they were at import. A board administrator can set the **Author** by hand, which is how an import that arrived before anyone had an account gets one afterwards.

The description is Markdown. A ticket that already has one opens with the rendered text; **Edit** (or a click on the text) brings back the editor. The **Save** beside the field stores just the description right away and shows the rendered result without closing the ticket; **Cancel** drops the unsaved changes and returns to the rendered view. A ticket without a description starts in the editor. Viewers see the rendered text only.

Cards are moved by dragging them between lanes or within a lane. On narrow screens each card offers a lane dropdown instead.

### To-dos and attachments

Each ticket carries a to-do list: record the next steps, reorder them, and tick them off. The **Add** button at the right below the list opens a small menu with *To-do*, *Decision*, *Subticket…* and *Comment*. *Decision* adds a question to settle rather than a step to do — which logo, which channels: type the question, add the answers (Enter adds the next one), choose *Single* or *Multiple* choice and, if wanted, *Allow “Other”* for an answer of one's own. Pick the answer and press *Decide*: the decision closes showing what was decided, what was not picked, and who decided it when; *Reopen* opens it again with the answers still picked. A decided decision counts as done in the traffic light, the card shows its result under the question, and the history records each decision and reopening. Setting it up and answering it are separate: a new decision opens for writing until *Done*, a saved one opens for answering, and *Edit* goes back to the question and its answers.

**Strict order.** Beside the *To-dos* heading, *Any order* / *Strict order* says how the list is worked. In strict order it goes top to bottom: every open step below the first open one shows a lock instead of its checkbox, a decision there cannot be decided yet, and the server refuses ticking one early. Undoing a step is always allowed. Subtickets tick themselves in their own lanes and are never locked, but an open one holds back the steps below it. Each subticket also waits on the subticket before it, exactly as if that were set under *Waits on*: the timeline draws the arrow and warns, and shifting the first moves the ones after it. Those dependencies are not stored — they follow the list as it is ordered now — and appear under *Waits on* with the list's number instead of a remove button. Switching back to *Any order* lifts all of it. The card marks a strict list with a numbered-list icon. *Subticket…* makes a step a ticket of its own instead. Under *New ticket* it asks for a title, a description, and the board and lane it lands on — this board or another one of the workspace you may edit; it gets the type "Ticket". Under *Existing ticket* it searches the open tickets of the workspace's boards you can read, by title or `#number`, and links the one you pick. The subticket shows in the list with its number, board and lane, opens with a click, and is ticked off by itself as soon as it sits in a lane marked Done on its own board; it cannot be ticked by hand. Removing it from the list only unlinks it, the ticket itself stays. A traffic light beside the count, in the editor and on the card, sums up the whole list, subtickets included: green when everything is done, orange from half done, red below that. Files such as screenshots and documents are attached directly to the ticket; images open in a lightbox. The files and links share a side column at the right of the ticket. Drag the handle in the middle of its left edge to widen it, up to the width of the comments column. The drawer grows by as much, so the fields and the comments keep their width; only a window too narrow for the wider drawer stops the drag earlier. Wider, the files and link cards sit side by side. The width holds for every ticket in this browser, and a double-click on the handle (or Home with the handle focused, the arrow keys in between) goes back to the narrow default. Each file is at most 25 MB, a ticket holds up to ten, and the file type has to be on the allowlist. Imported tickets work the same way: they take further files, and every attachment, including the screenshots, crash logs and Jira files an import brought along, can be deleted.

### Pictures on the card

With the picture switch in a lane's header on, its cards show pictures. Every image attachment and every link shown with a rendered preview carries a **Card** checkbox in the editor's side column; tick as many as the card should show. One picture fills the card's width; several share it in pairs, and past four the last one says how many more there are. As long as nobody ticked anything, the card shows one picture on its own: the oldest image attachment, or, on a ticket without one, the first link preview. Unticking everything is a choice too and leaves the card without a picture. A link switched back to plain text takes its preview off the card.

### Links

Links sit in the ticket's side column, stacked under the files: paste an address into the field below them, or type it and press Enter. A new link shows as its bare address. The picture button beside it (**Show with preview**) turns it into a card: a thumbnail of the page as a browser shows it, its title, and a line of its description from the page's Open Graph or Twitter tags. Only then is the page read and rendered, so a ticket full of plain links costs the server nothing. Rendering takes a few seconds and carries on when the ticket is closed; a toast says when the preview is ready, or that the page could not be rendered. Where the page cannot be rendered, the picture the page names for previews stands in, then the first photo of its content (menus, logos, icons, SVGs, and pictures too small or too stretched to be a photo are passed over), and finally a generic thumbnail with the host. The text button on a card (**Show as text only**) goes back to the bare address; another one renders the page again. Links carried over from the single link a ticket used to have, and the issue link a Jira import starts with, are plain addresses too. The card on the board shows the host of the first link and how many more there are.

### Assignment and discussion

A ticket can be assigned to any member of its board, and the assignee's avatar appears on the card. The **All assignees** filter next to the search box narrows the board to your own work, to a colleague's, or to everything nobody has picked up yet.

Each ticket carries a comment thread, open to viewers as well, and a history that records when it was created, moved, assigned, re-prioritised, archived, or restored, and through which agent when a token acted. Comments are Markdown like the description. Authors edit and delete their own comments; board administrators can also remove someone else's.

### Filtering and searching

The filter pane narrows the board by labels (any of the picked ones), category, or assignee, including everything still unassigned. The search box matches titles, descriptions, to-dos, authors, assignees, build numbers, links, Jira issue keys, and ticket numbers.

### Archive

**Archive** removes a ticket from the board without losing it, and an archived import is never imported again by a later sync. Editors archive; the archive itself belongs to the board's administrators. Only they see its icon in the header, reach the archive view, and restore from it. For everyone else an archived ticket is simply gone, including through its own address, and the dialog says as much before an editor archives anything.

## How it works

- **Roles.** Reading needs `viewer`; creating, editing, moving, and archiving need `editor`; restoring needs `admin`. An archived ticket answers 404 to anyone below admin.
- **Ticket numbers are instance-global**, so a number is a handle that works across boards and in a commit message. `ticket.getByNumber` needs no board.
- **Dependencies are a table of their own.** `ticket_dependencies` holds `blocker_id → blocked_id`, both cascading on delete. A ticket's `waitsOn` (`DependencyRef`s, ordered by due date) comes with every read; `ticket.create` and `ticket.update` take `waitsOn` as the whole list of ids and refuse with 422 a ticket itself, an archived one, one of another workspace, and one that already waits on this ticket through any chain (`waitsOnTransitively`). Changes land in the history as `waits_on`. The other side is `ticket.dependents.list` (`GET /api/tickets/{id}/dependents`, v1 `GET /tickets/{ticketId}/dependents`), filtered to readable boards. `ticket.subticket.candidates` with `for=dependency` offers what may be waited on. Board copies do not carry dependencies.
- **A recurring ticket is a milestone with a rule.** The column's check predates it, so `tickets.schedule` stays `milestone` and the `recurrence_*` columns (`ensureTicketRecurrence`) make it `recurring` on the way out (`domainSchedule`); `ticket_rounds` keeps one row per closed round with its outcome and the to-dos left open. `shared/utils/recurrence.ts` is the calendar. `closeRound` in `db.ts` closes a round and starts the next; `sweepRounds` runs it for every round whose next round has come, from the same plugin and interval as the live windows (`runRoundSweep`), and the row it writes is what keeps it from happening twice. `ticket.round.close` is the same by hand (`POST /tickets/{id}/round`, MCP `update_ticket` with `closeRound`). Each closed round keeps a `snapshot` of the lane, place and to-dos it closed on; `ticket.round.reopen` (`DELETE /tickets/{id}/round`, MCP `reopenRound: true`) puts them back through `reopenRound`, under the rule `reopenableRound` in `recurrence.ts` shares with the editor. It sends no webhook: it takes a close back, and the next close is sent again. A transfer to another board drops the return lane; duplicating a board maps it onto the copy.
- **The schedule is a column, settled in one place.** `tickets.schedule` is `none`, `milestone` or `lifecycle` (`ensureTicketSchedule` sorted existing tickets once: start equal to due without a live window became a milestone, keeping the day as due date; anything else dated a lifecycle). `settleSchedule` in `shared/utils/schedule.ts` works out the schedule and dates as they will stand for `ticket.create` and `ticket.update`: it clears what a switch has no room for, makes an undated ticket a lifecycle when it gets a date without a schedule, and refuses with 422 a date the schedule has no room for, a milestone without a day and dates on `none`. `createTicket` and `updateTicket` in `db.ts` apply the same fallback for callers that only know dates. The timeline draws a diamond for `schedule === 'milestone'` only (`isMilestone`).
- **Shifting is one operation.** `ticket.shift` (`POST /api/tickets/{id}/shift`, v1 `POST /tickets/{ticketId}/shift`) takes `days` and `dryRun`, walks the dependents breadth-first and moves start and due date in one transaction. It returns `moves` with before and after, and `skipped` with `finished` or `no_access`; a ticket the caller cannot read is skipped without its title. The timeline's dependency warnings, `starts_before_blocker` and `waits_on_late`, are `dependencyWarnings` in `shared/utils/timeline.ts`, used by the editor as well.
- **Where used is a read of its own.** `ticket.parents.list` (`GET /api/tickets/{id}/parents`, v1 `GET /tickets/{ticketId}/parents`) returns the parents from `ticket_todos` as `SubticketRef`s, filtered to boards the caller can read. MCP already has them as `parents` in `get_ticket`.
- **The timeline is a read of its own.** `ticket.timeline.get` (`GET /api/tickets/{id}/timeline`, v1 `GET /tickets/{ticketId}/timeline`) walks the subtickets from `ticket_todos`, skips archived ones, stops at a ticket that turns up among its own ancestors and after 500 nodes, and does not descend into boards the caller cannot read. The warnings are `timelineWarnings` in `shared/utils/timeline.ts`, shared by the editor and the MCP tools. `ticket.create` and `ticket.update` refuse a start after the due date with 422, checking an update against the dates the ticket already has.
- **Due date colours are computed, not stored.** `dueLevel` in `shared/utils/due-date.ts` compares a due date with today in Berlin time against the board's `dueWarningDays` and `dueAlertDays`, `liveLevel` the end of a live window against `dueWarningDays`; the card and `ticket.listDue` (behind the header alarm, `GET /api/tickets/due`) both use them. `listDueTickets` returns each ticket with `kind` `due` (open work, by its due date) or `live` (a live lane, by `liveUntil`). `/b/{board}?ticket=42` opens that ticket on arrival.
- **Positions are per lane.** `moveTicket` takes a lane and an index and renumbers the lane. `ticket.create` takes a `placement` (`top` or `bottom`, default `bottom`) and, for `top`, renumbers the lane the same way.
- **A decision is a to-do with `decision`** (`{ multiple, options, allowOther, chosen, other }`): `text` is the question, `completed` whether it is decided, `chosen` indexes into `options`. Stored in `ticket_todos.decision` (JSON) beside `decided_by` and `decided_at`. The schema refuses picks outside the options, more than one answer for a single choice, and a decided one without an answer (422). Since the list is rewritten as a whole, `setTicketTodos` matches each decision to the earlier one with the same question: an unchanged result keeps who decided it and when, a new one is stamped with the actor and recorded as `decided`, and an undone one as `reopened`. Over MCP an agent may ask and reword decisions but not decide them: `keepDecisionsOpenFor` in `server/operations/tickets.ts` carries the earlier result over, and starts new ones open.
- **Strict order is `tickets.strict_order`** (`strictOrder` on `ticket.create` and `ticket.update`, recorded as `strict_order` in the history). `checkStrictOrder` in `server/operations/tickets.ts` refuses with 422 a to-do or decision newly completed while one above it is open; only changes are checked. The implied dependencies are never stored: `ALL_DEPENDENCIES` in `server/utils/db.ts` adds, for every strict list, an edge from each subticket to the one before it (via `LAG` over `ticket_todos`), and every read of dependencies — `waitsOn`, dependents, cycle checks, the timeline and shift — goes through it. Such a `DependencyRef` carries `strictOrderOf`, the parent's number; `setTicketWaitsOn` does not store one that is sent back.
- **To-dos are replaced as a whole list** on `ticket.create` and `ticket.update`, up to 100 entries of 500 characters. A subticket entry carries `subticketId` and stays only when it is sent back with it; its `text` and `completed` are read from that ticket (its title, and whether its lane is a done lane), so whatever the caller sends for them is ignored. New subtickets come only from `ticket.createSubticket` (`POST /api/tickets/{id}/subtickets`, v1 `POST /tickets/{ticketId}/subtickets`), which asks for editing rights on the destination board too and refuses a board of another workspace; a to-do naming another ticket links it, provided the caller can read it (404 otherwise), it is open and on a board of the same workspace, and it is neither the ticket itself nor one that already lists it (422). `ticket.subticket.candidates` (`GET /api/tickets/{id}/subticket-candidates?query=`, v1 the same path) is the search behind the dialog. The light is `todoLight` in `shared/utils/todo-progress.ts`. Titles are at most 160 characters, descriptions 10,000.
- **Descriptions and comments are Markdown**, rendered by `renderMarkdown` with raw HTML switched off: a `<script>` in the text is shown as text, `javascript:` and `data:` links are dropped, and every link opens in a new tab with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`. That is what makes the output safe for `v-html` without a DOM-based sanitizer, so it works in SSR and in the node-only tests.
- **Links are replaced as a whole list** on `ticket.create` and `ticket.update` as `links: [{ url, display }]`, up to 50, http(s) only; `display` is `text` (the default) or `preview`. An address the ticket already had keeps its row and its preview, so re-sending the list does not read every page again, and a repeated address counts once. The single `link` field from before still works: on a ticket it reads as the first link, and setting it replaces the first link and keeps the rest. The old `tickets.link` column was copied into `ticket_links` once and is no longer read.
- **Previews are read lazily.** A new link is `pending`; the editor asks `POST /api/links/{id}/preview` only once the link is shown as a preview, so saves stay fast and an import never reads a hundred pages. The server follows up to four redirects for the page's tags, screening every hop like a webhook target (link-local always refused, private ranges per `WEBHOOK_ALLOW_PRIVATE`), and reads at most 768 KB of the page.
- **The thumbnail is a render.** `renderPageThumbnail` opens the page in headless Chromium (Playwright) at 1280×720 and keeps its first screen as a 640×360 JPEG beside the ticket's attachments. Every request the page makes — the document, redirects, scripts, images, frames, fetches — is screened exactly like the link itself, and WebSockets, service workers, downloads, pop-ups and dialogs are refused, so a page cannot point the server at the metadata service or the internal network. Consent banners of the common platforms are hidden, never clicked. One browser is shared, started on the first render and closed after a minute without one; two renders run at once. Without a browser (`LINK_PREVIEW_RENDER=false`, or none installed) the page's own image is cropped instead. SVGs are never decoded. The thumbnail is served from `GET /api/links/{id}/thumbnail` to whoever can read the ticket, never hot-linked: the content policy keeps images to this origin, and the remote site would otherwise learn about every view. Reading a preview again over an existing one (`refresh`) needs `editor`.
- **Categories and labels are set by name.** `categoryName` creates the category if needed; `labels` creates missing labels and prunes those left without a ticket.
- **The card's pictures are a list of ids.** `tickets.card_images` holds the ticked attachment and link ids as JSON, NULL while nobody ticked; `cardImages` in `shared/utils/card-images.ts` turns it into what the card shows, defaults included, for the card and the editor alike. A pick of which nothing is left on the ticket reads as unpicked. `cardImageIds` on an update replaces the pick (an empty list shows none, null goes back to the default) and takes only pictures `cardImageCandidates` offers. The older `coverAttachmentId` is still read and written as a pick of one, and the `cover_attachment_id` column keeps the first ticked attachment for an instance rolled back.
- **Attachments are validated twice**: by extension allowlist and by magic-byte signature, for uploads and for server-side downloads alike. Stored paths are resolved against the attachments directory and refused if they escape it.
- **Server-side download** (`attachment.addFromUrl`) screens the URL like a webhook destination, refuses redirects, and reads the body under a running 25 MB cap. See [mcp-server.md](mcp-server.md) for why it exists.
- **History stores people by reference.** Activity rows carry the actor's id and, for tokens, the agent label; names are resolved when rendered. See [users-and-access.md](users-and-access.md).
- **Paging.** `ticket.list` is unpaged for the UI and paged with a cursor on the REST surface, which defaults to 100 per page.
